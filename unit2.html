Core message · Unit 2
A blockchain replaces trust in an operator with trust in rules and incentives; it earns its cost only where no party should be trusted.
  • Blockchain combines cryptography, a shared ledger and a consensus mechanism so that strangers can agree on one record without a central keeper.
  • That agreement is slower, costlier and harder to change than a database run by a trusted institution, and many projects failed because they did not need it.
  • This unit trains students to explain how a blockchain works and to decide, with a structured test, when a problem really requires one.

Driving question

If six banks already trust each other enough to share a database, why would they need a blockchain?

Unit content (course outline)

  • Introduction to Blockchain Technology
  • Concepts of consensus, cryptography, and immutability
  • Types of blockchain: public, private, and consortium
  • Smart contracts and decentralized applications
  • Major blockchain platforms

AI integration in this unit

AI is used to explain and to test understanding: students ask an AI tool to explain proof of stake to a 12-year-old, then to a risk committee, and compare both answers with the Ethereum documentation. In the case, AI drafts a first pass of the 'Do You Need a Blockchain?' decision; teams must find where the draft skipped a question or assumed a fact not in the case. The decision and its justification stay human, and every AI contribution is declared.
