Teaching case
Tokenisation changes the plumbing of finance, not the promise; a token is only as good as the legal claim and the cash leg behind it.Core message · Unit 3 · ILO 1, 3, 4

Lombardia Real Estate Partners (LREP) is a Milan-based real estate investment manager. One of its funds owns a modern office building in the Porta Nuova district, valued at €40 million and fully let to three tenants on long leases. The building produces net rental income of about €1.8 million a year. The fund's units are held by twelve institutional investors, and the minimum ticket is €1 million.

LREP's managing partner, Giulia Ferrante, wants to open the asset to a wider investor base. Her idea is to issue digital tokens representing fractional economic interests in the building, with a minimum investment of €1,000. Rental income would be distributed quarterly to token holders automatically. Investors would be able to sell their tokens on a secondary market instead of waiting for the fund to be wound up.

Ferrante has spoken to three providers. The first is a German bank that operates a tokenisation platform on a permissioned ledger and holds a licence under the EU's DLT Pilot Regime, which has applied since March 2023 and allows market infrastructures to trade and settle tokenised financial instruments on distributed ledgers. The second is a fintech that would issue the tokens on a public blockchain using a widely used token standard with built-in transfer restrictions for verified investors. The third is a DeFi lending protocol that would accept the tokens as collateral, allowing holders to borrow stablecoins against them.

LREP's general counsel, Marco Rinaldi, has raised several questions. Are the tokens transferable securities under MiFID II, units in an alternative investment fund, or something else? Is a prospectus needed for a public offer to retail investors? What happens to the investor's rights if the platform fails? Who holds the private keys? The treasury team adds a further question: in what money will the tokens be paid for and the rents distributed? A euro stablecoin, a tokenised bank deposit and a link to central bank money via a trial settlement service each have different risks.

There is also international interest. A Zurich family office and two US investors have asked whether they could buy tokens. They would need to convert Swiss francs and US dollars into euros, and they want to know whether blockchain-based payment solutions could make this faster than the correspondent-banking route they use today.

Ferrante wants a proposal in two weeks: a token design that is legally robust, settles safely, is attractive to investors and does not expose LREP to risks it cannot control.

Your tasks

  1. Classify the proposed token with the Token Taxonomy and, for the US investors, the Howey test. What follows for the offer?
  2. Design the settlement: which cash leg would you use, and how does your design achieve delivery versus payment?
  3. Should LREP allow the tokens to be used as collateral in the DeFi lending protocol? Analyse with the overcollateralised lending model.
  4. How could a blockchain-based payment solution change the FX path for the Swiss and US investors, and what risks remain?
  5. Decision: choose one provider and present a token term sheet with the three risks you would disclose first.

Composite teaching case. LREP, its people and figures are invented. The EU DLT Pilot Regime (Regulation (EU) 2022/858) and MiCA (Regulation (EU) 2023/1114) are real.
