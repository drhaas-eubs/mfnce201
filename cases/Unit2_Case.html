Nordhaven Trade Ledger: Consortium, Database or Neither?

Teaching case
A blockchain replaces trust in an operator with trust in rules and incentives; it earns its cost only where no party should be trusted.Core message · Unit 2 · ILO 1

Nordhaven is an informal alliance of six mid-sized European banks — from the Netherlands, Germany, Belgium, Denmark, Austria and northern Italy — that together finance around 9,000 small and medium-sized exporters. Their customers ship machinery, food and chemicals across Europe and increasingly to Asia. Each sale creates invoices, purchase orders, transport documents and, often, a request for invoice financing or a payment guarantee.

The problem the banks want to solve is double financing. Twice in the last three years an exporter presented the same invoice to two Nordhaven banks and received financing from both. The combined loss was €14 million. Each bank checks its own records, but none can see what the others have financed. Paper documents and PDFs make it easy to present the same invoice twice.

A technology vendor has proposed the Nordhaven Trade Ledger: a permissioned blockchain on which each bank records a hash of every invoice it finances. Before financing, a bank would check whether the hash already exists. Smart contracts would release payment guarantees automatically when a shipping document is registered. The vendor estimates a build cost of €11 million and running costs of €2.5 million a year, shared among the members.

The board is cautious. Several bank-led blockchain platforms for trade finance closed between 2022 and 2023 after failing to win enough corporate users. Nordhaven's own head of IT argues that a shared database operated by a neutral company — for example a joint venture with a credit bureau — would solve double financing at a fraction of the cost. The legal team points out that registering a shipping document on a ledger does not prove that goods were shipped: someone still has to confirm it.

There are also governance questions. Who would run the validator nodes? What happens if one bank leaves or is taken over by a competitor? Could a new member join without the consent of all six? Two of the banks are competitors in the same regional market and are reluctant to let the other see their customer volumes, even in hashed form.

The board has asked a team of analysts to answer one question: does Nordhaven need a blockchain, and if so, what kind? It expects a structured argument, not enthusiasm.

Your tasks

  1. Work through the Wüst–Gervais decision tree for Nordhaven. Which answer to which question decides the outcome?
  2. Where does the oracle problem appear in the proposed design, and who would act as the oracle?
  3. Compare the permissioned blockchain with a shared database run by a neutral operator on cost, trust, governance and privacy.
  4. Where would you place the Nordhaven Trade Ledger on the Iansiti–Lakhani adoption matrix, and what does that imply for its timeline?
  5. Decision: recommend one option to the board and state the condition under which you would change your recommendation.

Composite teaching case. Nordhaven and its figures are invented. The closures of bank-led trade-finance platforms (we.trade in 2022, Marco Polo in 2023, Contour in 2023) are documented in the trade press.
