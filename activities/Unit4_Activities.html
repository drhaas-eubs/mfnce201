Role Play
Core message · Unit 4
Transparency is not accountability: a blockchain can show every transaction and still leave no one answerable when things go wrong.
  • Blockchain promises more efficient and transparent markets, faster cross-border payments and new products.
  • Those promises come with new risks — code failures, cyberattacks, regulatory gaps, energy use, exclusion and shifts in monetary sovereignty — that technology alone cannot govern.
  • This unit trains students to weigh efficiency gains against ethical and social costs and to propose solutions that law, markets, norms and code can hold to account together.
Intended learning outcomes addressed
3Discuss the theoretical factors influencing foreign exchange rates, the roles of currency markets, and their consequences on international corporate finance, all within the context of blockchain technology.
4Propose blockchain based solutions for improving market efficiency and transparency.
5Critically assess the ethical considerations and social implications of blockchain adoption in finance, foreign exchange, and securities markets.

Session question
Should the supervisor let Atlântico Pay launch a euro e-money token for remittances to Brazil, and on what conditions?
1
Briefing and role assignment
15 min

Present the case. Assign six stakeholder teams: issuer, supervisor, consumer association, incumbent bank, central bank, cybersecurity and sustainability auditor. Each receives a confidential role card.

2
Mini-lecture: efficiency, regulation, risk
25 min

EMH and transparency, MiCA categories, 'same activity, same risk, same regulation', NIST CSF 2.0 and the blockchain trilemma, applied to stablecoins.

3
Preparation with AI
30 min
  1. Use an AI tool to draft your strongest argument and the expected objection
  2. Verify every legal claim against the MiCA text
  3. Choose one framework from Slibrary 8 to anchor your ethical position

Output: Position card: argument, evidence, ethical anchor

Break · 15 minutes
4
The hearing
55 min

Opening statements (3 minutes each), cross-questions between stakeholders, and a final round in which each team proposes one condition for approval.

5
Supervisor's decision
15 min

The supervisor team deliberates in public and announces: approve, approve with conditions, or refuse, citing the arguments that decided it.

6
Debrief: Lessig's four modalities
25 min

Sort every condition proposed into law, norms, market or architecture. Which modality carried the decision? What remained unaccountable? AI declarations.

Deliverable: A two-page supervisory decision memo: decision, conditions sorted by Lessig's four modalities, the main efficiency gain, the main ethical risk, and an AI declaration.

Materials

  • Role cards for six stakeholder teams
  • MiCA extracts on e-money tokens (Moodle)
  • Laptops with an approved AI tool
  • Four-modalities sorting board (A2)

On-screen study only — copying, downloading and printing are disabled.
