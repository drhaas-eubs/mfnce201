Case Study
Core message · Unit 2
A blockchain replaces trust in an operator with trust in rules and incentives; it earns its cost only where no party should be trusted.
  • Blockchain combines cryptography, a shared ledger and a consensus mechanism so that strangers can agree on one record without a central keeper.
  • That agreement is slower, costlier and harder to change than a database run by a trusted institution, and many projects failed because they did not need it.
  • This unit trains students to explain how a blockchain works and to decide, with a structured test, when a problem really requires one.
Intended learning outcomes addressed
1Critically review the fundamental concepts and principles of blockchain technology and its potential applications in finance, foreign exchange, and securities markets.

Session question
Does the Nordhaven bank consortium need a blockchain, and if so, which kind?
1
Hook: the graveyard slide
15 min

Show the trade-finance platforms that closed between 2022 and 2023. Ask: technology failure or business failure?

2
Mini-lecture: how a blockchain works
30 min

Hash chain, public-key signatures, consensus and the Byzantine Generals Problem, demonstrated step by step with a blockchain explorer.

Output: Annotated block on the board

3
Hands-on: read a real transaction
25 min
  1. Open a public block explorer for Bitcoin or Ethereum
  2. Trace one transaction: inputs, outputs, fee, confirmations
  3. Find the previous-block hash and explain its role

Output: One annotated screenshot per pair

Break · 15 minutes
4
Case work: run the decision tree
50 min
  1. Read the Nordhaven case data
  2. Ask an AI tool for a first-pass answer to each Wüst–Gervais question
  3. Correct the AI where it assumed facts not in the case
  4. Choose: no blockchain, permissioned chain or public chain
  5. Place the use case on the Iansiti–Lakhani matrix

Output: Decision tree with answers and evidence

5
Board meeting
35 min

Teams present a 4-minute recommendation to the consortium board, played by the lecturer and one student per team. Board members challenge on cost, governance and the oracle problem.

6
Debrief and AI declaration
10 min

What did the AI get wrong? Which question in the tree decided the case?

Deliverable: A one-page board recommendation: the decision-tree path, the chosen architecture or alternative, the position on the adoption matrix and the two main risks.

Materials

  • Laptops with a public block explorer and an approved AI tool
  • Wüst–Gervais decision tree (A3)
  • Iansiti–Lakhani matrix (A3)
  • Nordhaven case handout
